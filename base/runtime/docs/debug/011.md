# 011 Frame limiter: catch-up bursts and a lost rate under load

Symptom: with the machine fully loaded, the first limiter tests gave a mean of 59.3 fps at a 60 fps target, p99 intervals of 59 ms, and intervals as short as 1 ms (240 fps case) right after a stall.

Cause: two things. The schedule was absolute (next = previous + period) and a frame up to four periods late was made up for in full, so a late wakeup was followed by a burst of back-to-back frames. And the timing cases ran at normal thread priority, so on a machine where other processes kept 12 to 16 of 24 logical cores busy the measuring thread was preempted for tens of milliseconds.

Fix: a late frame is made up for by at most 10 % of a period (the next interval is never shorter than 0.9 period, minus the lateness of the frame before it); a stall of more than four periods restarts the schedule. The timing cases run the thread at TIME_CRITICAL and the process at HIGH priority, like a game's render thread. Result under 100 % CPU load: 60.0000, 120.0000 and 240.0000 fps, p99 deviation 0.2 to 0.5 ms (`gfx_limiter`).

Also added: `FrameLimiter::Stats` separates the limiter's accuracy (lateness of frames that had to wait) from what the frame body costs (frames that overran), because a frame loop on a busy GPU is not the limiter's fault (see 009).
