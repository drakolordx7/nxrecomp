# S48: turning the morph ball camera with the mouse

Branch `ballcam`. In the morph ball, the mouse turns the camera around the ball, and so does the right stick. It is on by default. `[mouse] ball_camera = 0` turns it off, and `[mouse] ball_sensitivity` sets its speed (see `docs/CONFIG.md`). Both are also on the F1 page (Mouse tab) and on the launcher's Controls page.

## What was there before, and what was wrong

S13 already let the mouse orbit the ball camera, and S36 added the right stick. They turned the picture only: after the game moved its camera, the port rotated the camera around the ball, and before the next update it put the game's own camera back. The game never knew about the turn.

That broke rolling. The ball's controls follow the camera you see, so after a 90 degree turn "forward" rolled the ball sideways from the game's point of view. The game's camera follows the ball like a leash, so it swung round behind the new direction, the view turned with it, and holding forward rolled the ball in circles. Measured in the Frigate hangar with the old build: holding the stick straight forward for three seconds after a mouse turn, the ball's heading went from -0.30 to 1.73 to -3.13 radians.

## What the game does

Read in IDA (`CBallCameraMP1`, the same design as the GameCube `CBallCamera`):

- The ball camera keeps no angle of its own. Each update starts from where the camera is now and keeps it on that side of the ball at the set distance and height, with springs and its own wall handling.
- Nothing in it reads the right stick. Its input function (`CBallCameraMP1::ProcessInput`, 0xcffe34) only reads the chase camera button, and no other ball camera function reads an analog control. So Remastered's own controls give the right stick no job in the ball, as S36 also saw in the game.
- `CBallCameraMP1::TeleportCamera` (0xcf6d84) moves the camera, its smoothed position, its three rings of collision probes and its collision body in one go.

## How it works now

- Left and right: before the game updates its camera, the port moves it around the ball by the turn since the last update, through the game's own `TeleportCamera`. The game then carries on from the new side with all of its normal behaviour. The turn stays, and the ball rolls toward where you look.
- Up and down: the game resets the camera height on every update, so this part is still a turn of the picture around the ball, as before. It is kept between 5 degrees above the ball (not in the floor) and 80 degrees (almost overhead). If the game itself puts the camera past one of those limits, the offset only shrinks toward zero; it never pushes the camera the other way. It resets when you leave the ball. It does not change which way the ball rolls.
- Only the game's free ball camera turns. Rooms where the game holds the camera (fixed, path or spindle cameras, camera hints) and the morph and unmorph animations keep the game's camera, and a turn made then, up and down included, is dropped.
- Mouse counts go straight in: no smoothing, no acceleration. The stick turns at `[pad] ball_orbit` radians per second times the frame time. Both work every rendered frame at any frame rate.
- With `ball_camera = 0` the mouse and stick do nothing in the ball, and the game's own camera is left alone.

The code is `games/mpr/play_ballcam.cpp` (the maths and the checks) and the two hooks in `games/mpr/mpr_play.cpp` (`CPlayerMP1::ProcessInput` collects the turn, `CBallCameraMP1::Think` applies it). No new hook sites, so no new lift.

## Settings changes

- New: `[mouse] ball_camera` (default 1) and `[mouse] ball_sensitivity` (Source scale, default 2, both directions, 0.1 to 20 like the F1 slider; `morph_yaw` and `morph_pitch` take the same range in radians per count).
- `morph_yaw` and `morph_pitch` are still read. The game now writes `ball_sensitivity`, plus `morph_pitch` only when the vertical speed differs.
- The F1 page has a check box and one sensitivity slider in place of the two sliders. The launcher has a check box above its slider, and the slider greys out while the box is off.
- The base code calls these the "alternate body's camera" (`alt_camera`, `alt_sensitivity`); Metroid's names come from its vocabulary table.

## Not checked

- Rooms with fixed or scripted ball cameras (Tallon Overworld tunnels, half-pipes, spider ball tracks): the code hands them to the game, but no such room was played.
- Turning the camera into a wall: the game's own wall handling takes over after the move, but a turn that would put the camera inside a wall was not tried.
- A real mouse and pad in a window. Only the test script's mouse and virtual pad were used.
