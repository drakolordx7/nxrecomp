# S45: suit and arm cannon colour

Two optional settings, off by default, so the game looks as it does now:

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `[tweaks] suit_color` | `off` | `R,G,B` (0 to 255 each): Samus's suit is repainted in this colour, black to white. The morph ball and the spider ball follow it. |
| `[tweaks] gun_color` | `off` | `R,G,B`: the arm cannon's shell is repainted. |

The launcher's Tweaks tab and the F1 page's Tweaks tab have a switch, three sliders and a colour swatch for each, next to the glow colour, and a "Reset to the game's paint" button. A change shows in the game about a quarter of a second after the slider stops moving (no restart). The keys are in `docs/CONFIG.md`.

The visor glass, the green, cyan and red lights painted on the suit, the glow maps, the normal, metal and reflection maps are other textures or other colours and keep the game's look. The glow has its own setting (S44, `glow_color`).

## How it works

Samus's look comes from her base colour (`BCLR`) textures. They are ASTC in the game and BC7 on the PC (the setup tool transcodes them into `bc7_cache`). The tweak repaints them on the CPU when the game uploads them:

1. Which textures. A table of asset ids (below). The first time a colour is switched on, the game module reads those textures from the player's own paks (`Preload/DuplicateData.pak`, `Preload/MPR1/SamusGunMP1.pak`, `Preload/MPR1/TestAnimMP1.pak`) with the setup tool's pak reader and keeps the size, shape and a 64-bit hash of each tiled buffer. A texture the game uploads with the same size, shape and hash is that asset. Nothing from the game is stored or shipped.
2. Texture edit hook (base runtime, `include/nxrt/tex_edit.h`, `src/gfx/nvn/nvn_tex_edit.cpp`). For a matched texture, `prepare_texture` decodes every level of the ASTC data to RGBA8, hands each level to the game module, and encodes BC7 again (bc7e preset `[nvn] tex_edit_quality`, default 1). The edited data is kept in memory per (texture, colour), so a texture that is uploaded again costs a copy. Edited data never goes into `bc7_cache`: the shared cache stays the game's look, and switching the colour off goes back to it.
3. Live changes. When the colour has been still for 250 ms, the module bumps its generation and asks the hook to rebuild: a worker thread makes the new data of every live texture that matches and marks each one for upload as soon as its data is ready, so the render thread copies finished data instead of encoding. Textures edited before that no longer match (colour off) are uploaded again as the game's.
4. Paint mask. A smooth weight per texel from its hue, saturation and brightness (`games/mpr/mpr_tint.cpp`, profiles):
   * warm: red, orange and beige (hue 15° ± 40°, fading over 12° more), saturation from 0.12 to 0.25, brightness from 0.04 to 0.12. The Power and Varia suits, the ball, the Power and Varia cannon.
   * Gravity: warm plus purple (255° ± 30°).
   * Phazon: the light grey panels (saturation below 0.10 to 0.20, brightness above 0.30 to 0.45). Its red lights are saturated and stay.
   * teal: the beam gun's grey-green (160° ± 35°, saturation from 0.05).
   The green visor and lights, the cyan and lime lights, greys, metal and black seams have weight 0.
5. Recolour (OKLab). The reference paint of a texture is the chroma-weighted median lightness and the median chroma of its painted texels in level 0. Each painted texel keeps its lightness relative to that reference: below it, lightness scales with the chosen colour's lightness; above it, it scales toward white. Its hue and chroma become the chosen colour's, times the texel's own chroma against the reference chroma (0 to 1.25), so the paler panels stay paler. The chosen colour's lightness is held within 0.24 to 0.92, so black reads as a dark charcoal with its detail and white keeps its shading. The result is mixed with the game's texel by the mask weight, in linear light. Alpha stays.

The morph ball follows `suit_color`: the game paints the ball in the suit's colours (Power ball red and orange, Varia orange, Gravity purple), so a blue suit with an orange ball would look wrong. The ball glow keeps its own setting (`ball_glow`).

## Cost

Measured in the two in-game runs (RTX 3080 PC shared with other games, so the numbers are from a loaded machine; one edit = ASTC decode, recolour and BC7 encode of the whole mip chain, all CPU):

| Texture | One edit |
|---|---|
| 2048x2048 (suit bodies) | 220 to 720 ms, mostly 260 to 340 ms |
| 2048x1024 (arm cannon shells) | 120 to 200 ms |
| 1024x1024 (beam gun, ball) | 95 to 290 ms, mostly 110 to 150 ms |
| 256x256 | 45 to 140 ms |

* The game keeps all four suits, all four cannon shells and the balls loaded in the hangar, so a change rebuilds them all: suit colour 10 textures in 1.6 to 1.9 s, cannon colour 5 textures in 0.7 to 0.8 s, on a worker thread at below-normal priority. Each texture is uploaded as soon as its own data is ready. Switching a colour off costs nothing (the game's data comes from `bc7_cache` again).
* Reading the 15 textures from the paks to recognise them: 42 ms, once, on a worker thread, the first time a colour is switched on.
* The render thread only copies finished data; the matching costs a size compare per texture and one hash of the candidates of the right size.
* Memory: the edited BC7 data of the current colour, about 40 MB with both colours on and every suit loaded (estimate from the texture sizes).

## Checks

* CPU tests: `play_tint` (mask on paint and lights, paint reference, recolour to the chosen colour, shading order kept from black to white, grey and Phazon, the table, the keys and their round trip through the file text), `gfx_nvn_tex_edit` (the hook's edit reaches RGBA8 output texel for texel, kept data reused per generation, unmatched textures unchanged, edited BC7 never written to `bc7_cache` and the game's cache entry not used for an edited texture). Test filter: `play_tint|gfx_nvn_tex_edit|gfx_nvn_texdata|launcher_`.
* In game (done twice, shots checked by eye): one headless, muted live run at 720p with the pool copies off, New Game, then from the same spot the game's paint, black and blue, switched live through `NXRT_TINT_TEST_FILE`: the first-person cannon, the suit in third person during the morph, the morph ball. Result: the cannon shell and the beam gun turn charcoal black and blue with their ridges and shading, the yellow glow strips stay; the Power Suit turns black and blue all over (shoulders, helmet, arms, the yellow-green torso), the visor and the lights stay; the morph ball follows. The run log lists every texture recognised and each edit's time. Only the Power Suit was seen in game; the Varia, Gravity and Phazon textures were matched and edited in the same runs (they are loaded in the hangar) but only seen in the previews.

## Limits

* GPUs that sample ASTC themselves (some integrated GPUs) get the game's ASTC data unchanged: the tweak does nothing there (the hook only runs where the texture becomes BC7 or RGBA8).
* The mask is per hue, not per material: where the game painted a light in the same hue as the paint (an orange light on the Varia Suit), it takes the colour too.
