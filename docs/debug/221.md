# 221 Title stalls and a frame rate far under the cap: woken guest threads wait for the host scheduler

Symptom (S8): 100 to 250 ms stalls at the title screen, 96 and 90 fps at caps of 120 and 144. The stalls were not regular and varied a lot between runs: 7 stalls above 50 ms in one 30 s run, 58 in the next.

What the trace showed (frame trace, `[prof] trace`; one line per frame, plus every SVC and HLE call above 100 us and the render thread's zones): in a stall every guest thread sits in a wait, the render thread is idle, and nothing runs. One example: the guest render thread called `nvnQueueSubmitCommands` at t, the NVN render thread replayed it in 1.6 ms and signalled the guest thread, and the call returned 87 ms later. CPU time per thread during such a stall (`[prof] stall_ms` writes it) is a few milliseconds in total. The GPU is not involved: the title frame replays in 1.6 ms and the GPU runs it in 0.35 ms.

Cause: the console core model (debug note 180) pins each guest thread to one host processor, the top of the affinity mask (here three E-cores), and turns Windows' wake-up priority boost off. A thread that is woken (a condition variable, a futex wake, the hand-over of a guest mutex) cannot run until the scheduler gives it its one processor. With other processes busy that takes up to a quantum, and the game hands over between its threads dozens of times per frame. The busier the machine, the worse it gets. These measurements were taken while other builds and test runs loaded the machine to 50 to 90 %.

Controlled test: 24 busy-loop processes at normal priority (every logical processor at 100 %), title screen, cap 60, 30 s windows, `scripts/prof/prof_run.py --hogs 24`:

| mpr.exe | fps | p50 ms | p99 ms | stalls over 50 ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| normal class, E-core pinning (as before) | 34.4 / 32.5 | 30.7 / 32.5 | 61 / 83 | 38 / 65 |
| high class | 59.9 / 59.9 | 16.67 / 16.67 | 16.8 / 16.8 | 1 / 1 |
| high class, pinned to P-core threads (`host_cpus = 0x5555`) | 59.7 / 59.7 | 16.67 | 16.8 | 2 / 2 |
| high class, no pinning (`console_cores = 0`) | 59.9 / 59.9 | 16.67 | 16.8 | 1 / 1 |

The pinning itself costs nothing measurable at the title; the priority class is what decides. Above normal is not enough (the lowest guest band ranks equal to a normal process, debug note 180).

Fix: `kernel::sched_set_process_priority` (`[sched] priority_class`, default `auto`): play mode raises the process to the high class when it has a window; headless runs, tests and the boot loop stay at normal. The pinning and the unboosted wake-up are unchanged (the console model is the point of them). `NXRT_SCHED_PRIORITY_CLASS=normal` gives the old behaviour for comparisons.

The rest of the 120 and 144 shortfall was the same cause plus debug note 220; see S10.md for the numbers per cap. Remaining stalls on a shared machine come from the GPU: other processes on the same RTX 3080 show up as a `nvnSyncWait` that waits 10 to 20 ms for a 0.3 ms submission.
