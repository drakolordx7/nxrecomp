# S35: controller play

Branch `pad`. The right stick looks up and down again, the free lock-on turns the way the game's own turn does, and a pad now works through the whole game: menus, map, log book, morph ball, scan, lock-on, charge, missiles, rumble and gyro. Nothing in this work needed a new hook in the recompiled code, so no relift was done.

No real controller is attached to the test machine. Every pad check here ran through an SDL virtual gamepad that lives inside the game process (see "Testing without a pad"). It goes through the same SDL, mapping and game input path as a real one. What a virtual pad cannot show is listed at the end.

## What was wrong with the stick

The mouse aim hooks `CPlayerMP1::ProcessInput`. It kept its own pitch angle and, after the game's code ran, wrote that angle over the game's pitch and pitch target. The right stick works by moving the target, so every frame the stick's work was thrown away. Sideways looking worked because it goes through the body's turn, which the aim does not touch.

Now, while a pad is looking up or down (the right stick's vertical axis is off centre, or the gyro is moving), the aim leaves the pitch to the game and takes over the game's value as its own. The mouse still adds to it in the same frame, and the first frame after the stick is let go adopts the game's pitch once more. The last device used wins, nothing fights.

Checked in the Frigate hangar with the dual stick scheme:

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Stick down, then up, at 60 steps per second | Pitch goes to the game's limits (-1.22 and +0.87 rad) and stays where the stick left it |
| Mouse up 0.46 rad, stick up 0.16, mouse up 0.11, stick down 0.23 | Every step adds on top of the last one |
| Free lock, stick up and down | The view stops at +0.35 and -0.35 rad, the free lock margin |

## The game's own controls

Options, Controls in the pause menu has four control schemes (Dual Stick, Pointer, Hybrid, Classic), inversion for both axes, camera, aim and spring ball sensitivities and a few layout swaps. They all act on the pad as they do on a Switch, because the pad goes through the game's own code. Dual Stick is the default and is what the stick pitch above is about.

| Scheme | Pitch from the right stick | Gyro |
|---|---|---|
| Dual Stick | accumulates while the stick is held, stays when let go | none |
| Hybrid | eases back to level; R or ZR held turns the stick into a free look | R or ZR held = gyro camera |
| Classic | R or ZR held = free look, eases back to level | none |
| Pointer | pointer on the screen | pointer from the pad's orientation |

## Frame rate

In the dual stick scheme the game adds the stick's pitch to its target once per ProcessInput call, with no time step. At 60 steps per second that is 1.18 rad/s at full deflection. The same code at 144 gave 2.7 rad/s. The amount added also grows with the stick's deflection to about the power 2.2 (measured: 20, 50 and 90 percent give 0.5, 20 and 80 percent of the full step), so scaling the result is not an option: the game keeps a smoothing filter on that target and a rewritten target makes it drift. Instead the stick's vertical axis is scaled before the game reads it, by (step x 60) to the power 1/2.2, while the scheme is on.

| Steps per second | Pitch speed at full stick (rad/s) |
|---|---|
| 60 | 1.18 |
| 90 | 1.17 |
| 120 | 1.11 |
| 144 | 1.19 |
| 240 | 1.39 |
| 30 | 0.54 |

Below 60 the stick is already at its full range and cannot be scaled up, so 30 steps per second pitches at half speed. 240 is 17 percent fast because the game's response is not an exact power law. Rerun: `python pad_pitch_sweep.py 30 60 90 120 144 240` (needs the pad run in gameplay, see below).

The gyro look has the same fault and is not corrected: in Hybrid, the same turn of the pad pitches 2.7 times as far at 144 steps per second and 4.5 times as far at 240 as at 60. Its input is the sensor's angular rate and the game adds it once per call. The yaw does not have this, it goes through the game's physics.

## Free lock-on with the stick

The game turns Samus toward a stick by pushing her angular velocity toward the stick times the full turn speed. In the lock-on the game skips that, so S32 applied the full turn speed at once. It now ramps the way the game's own turn does. Measured with the body unlocked at 60 steps per second, full stick from rest: 47, 72, 85, 92, 96 percent of the full speed in the first five frames, and 45, 11 and 2.6 percent in the three frames after the stick is let go. Those fit an exponential approach with a time constant of 0.0262 s going up and 0.0117 s going down (`turn_ramp_step`, constants in `play_lockcam.cpp`). In the free lock the same measurement gave 0.706 of the 1.5 rad/s full turn speed in the first frame (47 percent), the same curve. The ramp is exponential in time, so it is the same at every frame rate.

The view still stops at the margin: yaw 0.58 rad (33 degrees) either side of the target, pitch 0.35 rad, as in S32.

## Gyro

`[pad] gyro` (default on) passes SDL's gyro and accelerometer to the game's six-axis sensor. Two things were added:

* the pad's orientation (`direction` and `angle` of the six-axis state), which the game's Pointer scheme reads and which was always the identity before. `pad_orient.cpp` integrates the gyro and pulls the tilt back to gravity slowly with the accelerometer.
* the hid service now takes those two from the input source instead of writing the identity.

Checked with a virtual gyro (SDL virtual sensor data):

| Scheme | What happens |
|---|---|
| Hybrid, R held | A gyro turn about the pad's x axis pitches the view, y and z turn it. Without R nothing happens |
| Dual Stick, Classic | The gyro does nothing, as in the game's own diagram |
| Pointer | The game asks for a calibration (passes), after that the view reacts to the gyro. The directions of the turn were not checked against a real pad |

`gyro_scale` multiplies the readings (0.1 to 4). Hybrid's R-held gyro camera is the part to try first with a DualSense or Switch Pro pad.

## Settings

New keys in `[pad]` (all documented in `docs/CONFIG.md`, on the F1 page and the launcher's Controller tab):

| Key | Default | What |
|---|---|---|
| `deadzone` | 0.10 | left stick dead zone (it was both sticks') |
| `right_deadzone` | 0.10 | right stick dead zone; a file with only `deadzone` gives the right stick the same |
| `invert_y` | off | right stick up looks down, in play only; adds to the game's own invert |
| `look_sens` | 1.0 | multiplies the right stick in play (0.25 to 3) |

Rumble (`rumble`, `rumble_scale`), gyro (`gyro`, `gyro_scale`) and the trigger point were already there. Checked in the game (fresh New Game, `invert_y = 1`, `look_sens = 0.5`): the stick pushed down looks up, and the pitch speed drops to about a quarter (0.0045 rad per frame against 0.0195), because the game's pitch response is roughly the square of the deflection. Sideways turning is linear and drops to half.

## Testing without a pad

* `keydown vpad attach xbox|ps4|ps5|switch`, `vpad axis lx|ly|rx|ry|lt|rt <value>`, `vpad button south|east|west|north|back|guide|start|lstick|rstick|lshoulder|rshoulder|up|down|left|right <0|1>`, `vpad gyro x y z` (rad/s), `vpad accel x y z`, `vpad detach` are script steps of the live test mode (`src/gfx/play/play_vpad.cpp`). The virtual pad needs a window run (`python live.py start --gfx window`), because SDL announces pads through the window's event pump.
* `pad.sh` (live driver for `game-build-pad`), `pad_cmd.py "line|line" ...` (send script lines and print the status), `pad_tap.py TAG btn ...` (tap buttons with a shot after each), `pad_matrix.py` (what each button changes), `pad_pitch_sweep.py` (pitch speed per frame rate).
* The game's own prompts need `NXRT_PROMPTS_DIR=<runtime>\assets\prompts` in a development run.

## Not verified, needs a real controller

* Real stick feel, dead zone and sensitivity values: the virtual pad sends exact values.
* Rumble on a real motor (the game's values do reach SDL's rumble call). A DualSense or Xbox pad may need different strengths; `rumble_scale` is there for it.
* Gyro: the scale of the sensor data (SDL gives rad/s, the console rev/s), axis directions, Pointer scheme direction, drift. Gyro look frame-rate scaling is not done.
* Hybrid and Pointer feel with a pad held the usual way.
* Grapple: the Frigate has no grapple point, so it was not tried (the lock-on code is the game's own).
* The morph ball camera did not move with the right stick in the hangar (the ball's camera follows the ball by itself in the dual stick scheme); the mouse orbit is a PC feature of S13.

## Known

* 30 steps per second pitches at half speed with the stick (see Frame rate).
* The gyro look follows the frame rate above 60 steps per second.
* The stick scale's exponent (1/2.2) is a fitted number, not read from the game.
* The timing tests (`gfx_limiter`, others on the list in the status file) can fail on a loaded machine.
